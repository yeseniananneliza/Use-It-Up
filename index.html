    About this project

    I built Use It Up as a scoped product case study. It answers one question at the moment people decide what to eat: what can I make with what I already have?

    The problem

    People overspend on groceries and throw food away, not because they lack recipes, but because nothing connects the recipes to what's actually in their fridge. Recipe sites start from the dish. This starts from the kitchen.

    Target user

    Students and early-career professionals cooking for one or two. They have ingredients at home but struggle to decide what to cook before those ingredients expire.

    What v1 does

    It ranks recipes by how many ingredients you already have and moves recipes that rescue soon-to-expire food to the top. The grocery list combines what's missing across every recipe you plan to cook, so you don't buy garlic three times.

    What I cut, and why

    Barcode scanning, price tracking and a live recipe API all need external services and would have delayed testing the core loop. The grocery list started on the "later" list and moved into v1 once it was clear it was pure logic with no dependencies. Your kitchen is saved in your browser, so it's still there when you come back.

    Validation and learnings

    Testing in progress. I'm running short, moderated sessions where people open the site cold and plan a dinner from their own fridge. I'm tracking four questions:

    1. How long it takes to open a recipe they'd actually cook.
    2. Whether "Use soon" makes sense without an explanation.
    3. Whether the combined grocery list catches items they'd otherwise buy twice.
    4. Which step causes the most hesitation.

    One change came from reviewing the first version: it opened with a kitchen already filled in, so it wasn't clear what was the user's and what was a demo. First-time visitors now choose between a sample kitchen and an empty one.

    How I'd measure success

    The share of sessions where someone opens a recipe, the share of people who mark at least one ingredient "Use soon," the share of recipe views that lead to a grocery-list addition, and the share of people who come back to a saved kitchen.

    What's next

    Swap the hand-written dataset for a recipe API, add accounts so a kitchen follows you across devices, and measure whether people who mark food as "use soon" actually cook it.
